Sannomaru Takigi Noh — White Heron in the Dark

The 55th Himeji Castle Takigi Noh / At Sannomaru Square

Season
Spring
Time of day
Night
Date
22 May 2026
Gear
Leica M10 + Summilux 50mm
Location
Sannomaru Square, Himeji Castle

The opening night of the Himeji Castle Festival — the 55th Takigi Noh. With the White Heron Castle bathed in blue as backdrop, firelight and Noh weave a view that exists for just one night.

The Story

Every May, the Himeji Castle Festival opens with Takigi Noh — Noh performed by firelight in the open. 2026 marked the 55th edition. The programme: Noh plays "Kochō" (Butterflies) and "Tenko" (Heavenly Drum), and the kyōgen "Kitsunezuka" (Fox Mound). Curtain at 6 PM, on a temporary stage in Sannomaru Square.

The stage proceeds solemnly. The audience holds its breath; even the click of a shutter feels intrusive. Bonfires crackle, chant fills the dark. Tripods are not allowed.

For a photographer, this scene is a trial. A castle lit in blue, shifting firelight, performers moving through darkness — packing that dynamic range into a single frame means wrestling with nighttime ISO. Look closely at this shot and you'll see the performers are slightly blurred. Freezing them would have killed the atmosphere, so I let go. Night Noh, I suppose, is that kind of thing.

And yet — the same scene, captured on a Xiaomi 17 Ultra, comes out cleanly composed. The AI quietly handles the blur and the noise. The night I admitted, honestly, that phones had come this far.

A white castle, glowing blue, looking down at the fire. Thirty-Six Views of the White Heron, No. 34.

Photo Tips

  • 📍LocationSannomaru Square, Himeji Castle
  • 🤝EtiquetteA deeply solemn ritual. Be mindful of shutter noise, keep conversation to a minimum, and note that tripods and monopods are not allowed.
  • 📷Photo TipCastle, fire, and moving performers create an extreme dynamic range — freeze the motion and the image goes dark; preserve the atmosphere and it blurs. AI night modes on phones (e.g. Xiaomi 17 Ultra) handle it remarkably well.
